The Rae Lakes Loop is reputed to offer some of the finest splendor in the high Sierra. I like loop trips, and also splendor, and as of fairly recently, backpacking, so I decided to give this one a try, and in late July 2024 I ventured it as a 4 day, 3 night solo backpacking trip.

<!--more-->

There a few approaches to this area. One can start at Onion Valley on the east side of the range in Inyo National Forest and go over Kearsarge Pass. Or on the west side at Road's End, in King's Canyon, heading either clockwise (starting north from Road's End up Wood's Creek) or counterclockwise (starting east up Bubb's Creek). Clockwise means days of steady climbing, followed by a sharp descent. Counterclockwise is...the opposite. The only permit I could find on short notice was for Bubb's Creek, so that's the way I went! And, yep, that first day is indeed brutal. 

I originally planned to car camp the night before at Convict Flat, because Sentinel Campground — the only one still operational in Cedar Grove, the rest having been taken out of commission by damage from recent heavy winters — was fully booked. But I signed up for Recreation.gov availability alerts for it, and they let me know at the last minute that a spot had opened up, as evidently often happens right at the cancelation deadline. 

Drove up on Wednesday, through Fresno, up into the park, and back down on Hwy 180, past the confluence of the Middle and South forks of the King's River, to Cedar Grove. After a brief dialogue with the ranger at the permit station — "Do you have a bear canister? Do you have satellite comms? Do you promise not to press the SOS button if you're not in mortal danger?" — I was issued my paper permit. He seemed exhausted from delivering the same spiel over and over, but I suspect there's no substitute for having a human authority figure give it. 

I ambled around the facilities in Cedar Grove, checked out the hotel, the snack bar, and the exhibits in the visitor center. Some car tourists asked the ranger "how far can we go east?" and learned about Road's End. Struck me that, to them, it truly was the end, and beyond there might as well not exist. The National *Park* vibes always mesh awkwardly with their adjacent *Wildernesses*, and King's Canyon is no exception. So I took a little day hike up Hotel Creek to get away from the RV crowd and off the canyon floor for a better view. Startled some quail, saw smoke from a smoldering lightning-strike fire in the distance. Confirmed that Cedar Grove smells powerfully of cedar. Then spent a humid tent night at the uncomfortably populous campground, and early on Thursday morning drove back to Road's End, packed up, and set out.  

I moved fast, appreciating the fruits of my recent pack-lightening gear upgrades. Tentless this time; just a tarp and bivy, as the savvy hikers recommend. Even the first few miles of this hike offer spectacular mountain vistas around every bend. Not too far up Bubbs Creek, I passed a trail crew, one of whom called my attention to a bear just north of the trail, ambling along, "doing bear stuff." My first bear sighting, as it happens. I had somehow not yet seen one on my previous trips. Can confirm: they are quite charismatic. And nice to see my first one doing good healthy bear stuff, snuffling at berries and whatnot, rather than unhealthy campground picnic basket stuff. 

Crossing paths with hikers going the opposite way, it struck me: we are two complete mental cosmoses glancing off each other and continuing in opposite directions. Being up in the mountains, especially in the meditative frame I seek to cultivate for myself up there, to be simply and barely aware of each thought, the mindseams loosen, and it becomes easy to imagine simply swapping minds completely with a passerby, or a bear, or a hummingbird. Who's to say we don't?

A few miles west of Vidette Meadow, I came across a couple young Canadians walking west, in some distress. They asked me for help orienting themselves. They had come down from Forrester Pass, after parting ways with their friends who were continuing up to Whitney, and were aiming to catch a bus in Onion Valley later that day. They were very much *not* going the right way to Onion Valley. I showed them where we were on my Harrison map, and helped them deliberate — continue west to Road's End and take their chances with transportation. Hitchhiking, presumably. Or backtrack up (and up and up) to Kearsarge. They chose east. Walked 20 feet. Then turned around, too demoralized to backtrack. I hope they made it to LA! And I hope they bring a map next time.

I continued east, past Vidette Meadow, then turned north, up and up. Seeing faces in the rock faces, becoming perhaps slightly delirious from the elevation and exertion. A Schubert impromptu on a loop in the brain. After 12 hours of walking, under raindrops that thankfully stayed sparse, I arrived and set up camp right below Glen Pass. First time under a tarp out there, I struggle with sleep system setup and slept fitfully, rockfall echoing periodically through the cirque.

Friday morning, awoken by a hummingbird. Lots of them on this trip, feeding on the the [Penstemon](https://www.inaturalist.org/observations/232941391).
