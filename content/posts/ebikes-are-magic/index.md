I thought about getting an ebike for a few years before I finally pulled the trigger. With almost two thousand miles on the odometer, I'm happy to report that these are utterly magical machines, and if you're even remotely considering buying one, I'm pretty confident you won't regret that choice.

<!--more-->

Here are nine observations, some obvious, some subtler:

### 1. Often the *very fastest* way to travel

An ebike is simply the very fastest way to get to any destination within a handful of miles.  There's a market a mile from my house that I sometimes walk to. It's a nice walk! But it's 20 minutes each way — 40 minutes of walking back and forth. On my bike, it's maybe 3 minutes away. Locking up is quick — much faster than parking. Door to door just feels startlingly fast, almost a magic trick. 

### 2. And the *cheapest* way too, by a wide margin

Driving is quite costly per mile, especially when you include all the easy-to-ignore costs like insurance, aintenance, and depreciation. Public transit is cheaper, but far from free. But the marginal cost per mile traveled on an ebike is close to nil. Electricity to charge the batter is a rounding error. So if you score your options on cost & speed together, the ebike will often trounce your other options even more handily.

### 3. A more affordable purchase than you might think

I think many people have a misconception lodged in their minds that an ebike is a roughly five thousand dollar purchase. It certainly can be, but you don't need to spend anywhere near that to get a really solid one. Mine — a solid, dependable, well-made Class 3 bike with a throttle — is currently on sale for $1295.

### 4. Going faster cuts stress dramatically

When there's no adequate bike lane, the center of the rightmost lane is usually the best option to avoid the door zone. On my old acoustic bike, I used to find this deeply stressful. I could go pretty fast, but not fast enough to mollify drivers. If you ride at 15mph in front of them, drivers become psychopaths. I stopped riding entirely for a few years after some close-call punish-passes and verbal assaults. For me, a Class 3 ebike almost completely removes this discomfort. Because driver rage isn't linear with delay. At 25mph some drivers are still impatient, but they don't respond with with blind and incandescent rage. This may be the single biggest improvement in my riding experience compared to my old bike.

### 5. Quick acceleration forestalls many dangers 

Using the throttle to get up to speed when the light turns green, (or before it turns, at intersections with [leading pedestrian indicators]()) eliminates even more conflicts with impatient drivers. Because even on main arteries where people drive faster than I can go, the next stop is often near enough that a head-start often moots the issue before it arises. More directly, in situations like unprotected crossings, a throttle gets you through and out of the line of oncoming traffic, or whatever the danger might be, in a fraction of the time it takes on an acoustic bike.

### 6. A throttle is useful for *slow* maneuvering too

A subtle thing, but I often find myself wanting to inch forward a bit from a standstill to get a better view or maneuver past an obstacle. This can be annoying on acoustic bikes and ebikes without throttles. You may have to shift to an easier gear or reposition the pedals to get some leverage. A throttle makes it easy. You can lightly tap on it and roll forward a few inches. This is a surprisingly useful ability to have.

### 7. Pairs well with public transit

This depends on where you happen to live of course, but in the SF Bay Area, BART plus ebike works really well. A remarkable turnabout,since bikes on BART used to be completely banned. In x they became allowed, and the new generation train cars have very functional rails to strap them to (after an unfortunate and now abandoned attempt at a more complex solution). More recently, the pointless rule against taking them on escalators was repealed — thank you []. So you can usually avoid the slow and often malodorous elevators. My bike even has a walk-assist option that spins the motor at low RPM, that I can use to roll the bike up the rail on the sides of some stairwells, or even in a pinch (if somewhat clunkily) up the stairs themselves. 

I do wish Google Maps would add an option for dual-mode bike/transit trips though.

### 8. Integrated lights are a major convenience

Many ebikes have integrated lights that run off the same battery as the motor. That means you never have to think about charging them or remembering to bring them. And because they don't serve any function without a power source, it means they're not really worth stealing, so you don't have to bring them with you when you lock up. Never having to think about this is a significant convenience.

### 9. Highly exhilirating

If you've ridden a bike, you might think you know what it's like to ride an ebike. But if you've never tried one, you don't know. It's a categorically different experience, and you really ought to try it. Riding one of these things is just incredibly fun. A class 3 ebike is definitely a bike, but it offers some of the same exhiliration of riding a motorcycle, at much lower risk. Not everyone enjoys going fast, but if you're a confident cyclist and you like speed, I promise that the riding experience will put a smile on your face. 

---

So here's my advice: if you have even a glimmer of a desire for an ebike, test ride one. (If you live in the Bay Area you can test-ride mine!). You might be surprised by how much sheer fun they are, over and above their extreme practicality. And when you make your purchase, consider mail-order options like Ride1Up — they are an incredibly good value. Prioritize integrated lights and a throttle. And if you like going fast, choose Class 3.
