I've been reading a lot about legislative efforts to address our catastrophic housing scarcity problem, and found myself struggling to keep track of all the attempts. I figured someone must have made a database of them all. But evidently not! So I made one:

<!--more-->

[YIMBYpedia](https://yimbypedia.org) collects and categorzes YIMBY legislation. I seeded it with a few dozen entries from 15 states. 


A few scattered observations from surveying at least a slice of the legislative landscape: 

 * [Scott Wiener](https://yimbypedia.org/sponsors/scott-wiener/) is really a singularly productive leader on this front. But I'm also heartened to see [so many](https://yimbypedia.org/sponsors/) legislators getting in on the action.
 * There's a recurring whack-a-mole pattern where states make some salvo towards preempting local NIMBYism, then cities and counties find ways to ignore and subvert those efforts, then the state tries to close the loopholes, sometimes over several rounds. I wonder if this pattern could be short-circuited in the drafting process. [AB1820](https://yimbypedia.org/bills/ab1820/), mandating up-front fee clarity, for instance, is great. But I think it needs some kind of enforcement mechanism. Like maybe if they are not adequately detailed up-front, they can be deemed null and uncollectable via a speedy and simple appeal process.
 * The whack-a-mole pattern is particularly maddening because the states are in fact *sovereign* on matters of land use. Sub-state-level political entities — cities, counties — have only the power over the states deign to grant them. If they prove themselves to be inveterately subversive of duly enacted statewide policy, they ought to simply lose that autonomy entirely. Especially in that light, it's disappointing to see nothing on the agenda this year in California that seems likely to unleash homebuilding at any significant scale. What stars need to align for another go at something like [SB50](https://yimbypedia.org/bills/sb50/) and [SB827](https://yimbypedia.org/bills/sb827/)?
 * Frustratingly, I read a lot of bills that I suspect will in practice produce approximately no new homes at all! Something I'd like to see is a YIMBY thinktank with a staff of economists and researchers who can build expertise in quickly evaluating policy proposals to estiamte how many homes proposed legislation will *actually in practice* yield in, say, a 5 year window vs. the base case. Bills for which the answer rounds to zero, whether because they're too anemic, or because they impose just as many new barriers as they remove barriers, are a waste of time and political capital. 
 * Federalism! Really interesting to see the different approaches being taken in different states. Like [HB14](https://yimbypedia.org/bills/hb14/) in Texas last year, allowing for *third parties* to step in when local officials dilly-dally on approvals — is such a Texas way of going about this. I kinda love it. 

I'll continue adding to it as I come across legislation being discussed. But making this comprehensive would require more pairs of hands. So submissions are welcome [here](https://forms.gle/gbAsHz34VQiGBz1j6). You can also contribue to the codebase directly at [github.com/sjforman/yimbypedia](https://github.com/sjforman/yimbypedia). If people find this useful, I may try to make it directly editable, a proper wiki.
