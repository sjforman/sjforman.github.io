---
title: "Parking should never be free, but it should be easy"
date: 2024-04-19

Most people hate paying for parking and fiercely resist attempts to make them do so. I wonder how much of that resistance isn't to cost *per se*, but rather to the annoyance of the process of paying.

<!--more-->

One of my minority viewpoints is that I actively embrace paying for parking. I'm convinced that it shouldn't be free, and that making it mostly free (and often mandatory to build) is disastrous public policy. Which is just to say that I'm a [Shoupista](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Donald_Shoup). Donald Shoup was right about everything, and [The High Cost of Free Parking](https://amzn.to/3Qctj2p) is essential reading. Embrace Shoup and you get to feel good when you feed the meter. 

What I don't appreciate is the *actual process of paying*. The user experience of it. I think it's more important than generally credited. Even now that you can mostly pay with a credit card or an app, it's still a really annoying process. You bend over and squint at the little vandalized screen and struggle to read the tiny low-contrast text. You get the little slip of paper and have to walk back to put it in the windshield, or else type the code for the spot into the shitty app. 

Here's how it ought to work: the car knows where it is, and the transaction is automatic. You park, a timer starts running. You leave, you come back, you drive away, and your account is charged automatically for the exact amount of time you parked. No guessing how long you'll be there or futzing with the meter or kiosk. Like EZ-Pass. You get a statement at the end of the month, and if you don't pay, you can't renew your registration.

If it could be made to work this way, I suspect at least some resistance to Shoupthought would be abated. It also seems like enforcement and physical infrastructure could be significantly cheaper.

How could this work? Well first of all, another unpopular opinion, transponders should be mandatory for all licensed vehicles. That would make easier to build infastructure for this kind of thing, and also to deal with the epidemic of missing and fake plates. But even without that, cars are increasingly just laptops on wheels. They have GPS, they know exactly where they are. Software to handle this should be mandatory, with a clearly-defined interface that cities and states can hook into. It would be an administratively complicated thing to arrange, but I think it would be worth doing. And if autonomous cars become the norm, it will be essential.
